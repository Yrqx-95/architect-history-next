# Archistory Direction Report

## What Archistory is becoming

Archistory is moving from a pure architecture archive into a hybrid architecture learning system: part historical archive, part student knowledge base, part building-code and technical glossary. The current curation pool confirms that the strongest organic center is **architecture student knowledge**, with planning, history, structure, materials, and code forming the most useful learning spine.

## Balance assessment

| Product identity | Current strength | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture Archive | Strong | Existing public architecture/history content plus many history candidates. |
| Building Code Learning | Moderate | Code terms exist, but require official verification before expansion. |
| Architecture Student Knowledge Base | Strongest opportunity | A/B pool covers planning, drawings, structure, materials, construction, and history. |
| Interior Design Learning | Underdeveloped | Thin interior/furniture/lighting coverage and many product/name risks. |
| Exam Preparation Platform | Possible but risky | Exam-derived terms exist, but raw exam phrasing should not define the product. |

## Overdeveloped areas

- Proper-name history and individual architectural works relative to general concepts.
- Planning/history compared with environmental systems, accessibility, acoustics, and presentation.
- Candidate volume relative to reviewed, publishable definitions.

## Underdeveloped areas

- Environmental systems and building science.
- Accessibility and universal design.
- Acoustics.
- Interior design method, FF&E, color, presentation, and portfolio literacy.
- Construction sequencing and practical detailing.

## Largest opportunities

1. Build a structured architecture-student knowledge base around glossary clusters.
2. Promote high-confidence B terms into reviewed draft glossary entries.
3. Add environmental systems and accessibility as new learning pillars.
4. Separate archive entities, product/design-object entities, and glossary concepts.

## Largest risks

- Turning the glossary into a mixed bag of names, brands, exam fragments, and concepts.
- Publishing code or structural content without authoritative review.
- Expanding extraction faster than editorial capacity.
- Building UI for content that has not yet reached a stable editorial model.

## What NOT to build next

- Do not build more extraction/OCR workflows right now.
- Do not add product catalogs directly into the glossary.
- Do not create new public glossary pages before editorial review.
- Do not make exam-prep the main product identity before verifying source quality and pedagogy.
- Do not add new UI routes just to expose unreviewed candidates.

## What SHOULD be built next

- An internal editorial review workflow for B-to-A promotion.
- A glossary style guide covering Japanese canonical names, readings, Chinese/English translation rules, aliases, and entity boundaries.
- Learning clusters for structure, materials, building code, planning, and environmental systems.
- A separate model for people, buildings, institutions, brands, and products if those are strategically important.

## Highest ROI category

The highest ROI content category is **Architecture Student Knowledge Base**, specifically B-to-A promotion in structure, materials, planning, drawing, and building-code concepts. It uses the existing candidate pool, improves learning value quickly, and avoids the cost of new extraction.
