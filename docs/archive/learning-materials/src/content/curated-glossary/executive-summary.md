# Executive Summary

Archistory's knowledge layer is in a healthy but unfinished state. The current curated pool contains **596 unpublished candidates**, split into **74 A-grade draft terms**, **449 B-grade review terms**, and **73 C-grade rejects**. This is enough to support product decisions without more extraction. The immediate bottleneck is editorial review, not source discovery.

## 1. Current knowledge coverage

Coverage is strongest in architectural history, planning, structure, materials, spatial design, and building code. It is weakest in environmental systems, accessibility, acoustics, color, presentation, and practical interior-design workflows. Archistory is already more than an archive; it is becoming an architecture student knowledge base.

## 2. Glossary health

The glossary direction is healthy because A-grade terms are reusable, source-backed, trilingual, categorized, and still marked as drafts. The main risk is boundary control: proper names, products, exam fragments, and general concepts must remain separated.

## 3. Candidate pool health

The candidate pool is useful but noisy. A high B-grade count is expected and not a failure: many terms are valid but need definitions, translation review, canonical naming, or technical verification. C-grade rejections show that product names and proper names were correctly filtered away from the general glossary.

## 4. What can be promoted immediately

A capped shortlist of **140 B-grade terms** looks like plausible false negatives for the next editorial pass. A first editorial pass should start with terms blocked only by missing definitions or translation uncertainty. Examples include: 通り庭, 家具配置, 造作家具, エフロレッセンス, 減水剤, 高炉スラグ骨材, 実積率, 重量骨材, 線膨張率, 早強ポルトランドセメント, 単位水量, 中庸熱ポルトランドセメント, 低熱ポルトランドセメント, 反応性鉱物, 普通骨材, 家事室, 降伏点, 末端部フック, アクロポリス, アゴラ, アトリウム, イオニア式, イスラム建築, ヴォールト, エジプト建築.

## 5. What should be reviewed next

Review B-grade terms in this order: missing-definition terms, translation-uncertainty terms, duplicate/alias decisions, then technical-review terms in structure, materials, and code. Leave source-uncertain case-study phrases and low-value context fragments for later.

## 6. What source materials should be imported next

Do not import immediately. After B-to-A review capacity is established, the highest-value future sources are environmental systems textbooks, accessibility/universal design standards, structural design textbooks, building materials textbooks, and construction technology books. Product catalogs should wait.

## 7. Largest knowledge gap

The largest strategic gap is **environmental systems plus accessibility**. These areas are central to modern architecture education and almost absent from the current pool. Acoustics, color, and presentation are also critical gaps.

## 8. Next 90 days

Focus the next 90 days on editorial infrastructure and promotion, not extraction. First, finalize a glossary style guide and canonical naming rules. Second, promote the highest-confidence B terms into A-grade drafts. Third, build learning clusters around planning, structure, materials, drawing, code, and environmental systems. Fourth, define separate content models for glossary concepts, buildings, people, products, and exam questions before exposing more public content.

## Founder decision

Archistory should position itself as an **architecture student knowledge base built on top of an architecture archive**. The highest ROI is not more scraping; it is turning the current candidate pool into trusted, teachable knowledge.
