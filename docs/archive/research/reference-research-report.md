# Archistory Reference Research Report

## Purpose

This Phase 1 document defines the initial reference hypotheses to be tested through live-site research in Phase 2. It is not a substitute for the evidence-based benchmark sprint.

The selected references are product systems rather than architectural media sites:

- Linear
- Notion
- Khan Academy
- Google Arts & Culture
- Are.na

## Linear

### What appears relevant

- disciplined alignment
- dense but controlled navigation
- clear distinction between global navigation and work context
- restrained use of surfaces
- predictable hierarchy across complex views

### What Archistory may adapt

- consistent page edges and sidebar widths
- compact navigation with clear active context
- high information density without treating every item as a card
- explicit distinction between primary work and supporting metadata

### What should not be copied

- software-project vocabulary
- productivity-dashboard identity
- command-heavy interaction as the default
- dark, technical visual styling applied wholesale

## Notion

### What appears relevant

- readable content hierarchy
- calm typography
- flexible but recognizable content blocks
- progressive disclosure
- persistent navigation around long knowledge documents

### What Archistory may adapt

- strong title-to-body relationship
- predictable block spacing
- lightweight tables of contents
- knowledge pages that remain readable despite mixed media

### What should not be copied

- blank-document feeling
- excessive user configuration
- generic workspace identity
- deep nested page trees without curation

## Khan Academy

### What appears relevant

- obvious course starts
- units and lessons with progression
- clear next-step guidance
- beginner-oriented orientation
- mastery structure

### What Archistory may adapt

- one recommended starting route
- visible path and stage context
- continue-learning actions
- clear separation between path, unit, and learning item

### What should not be copied

- school-grade framing
- score-centric motivation
- exercise-first identity
- exam pressure and completion gamification

## Google Arts & Culture

### What appears relevant

- visual archive discovery
- thematic collections
- relationships across works, creators, places, and time
- immersive but accessible cultural storytelling

### What Archistory may adapt

- curated entry points into a large archive
- visual pathways from object to context
- thematic and historical exploration
- archive entities used as learning evidence

### What should not be copied

- spectacle without a clear learning next step
- media-heavy pages that obscure structure
- broad cultural scope without a defined learner journey

## Are.na

### What appears relevant

- networked knowledge behavior
- non-linear discovery
- preservation of source context
- user understanding through collections and relationships

### What Archistory may adapt

- visible conceptual connections
- multiple routes through the same entity
- collections as knowledge structures
- calm presentation of heterogeneous material

### What should not be copied

- ambiguity for first-time users
- dependence on user-created organization
- weak default learning sequence
- minimal context that assumes prior familiarity

## Phase 2 Research Questions

The live benchmark sprint must verify:

1. How quickly each site communicates its core purpose.
2. How global and local navigation are separated.
3. How dense pages preserve hierarchy.
4. How users are told what to do next.
5. How entity relationships are exposed.
6. Which patterns work without authentication.
7. Which patterns preserve cultural and archival identity.

Every Phase 2 conclusion must include a live URL, page observation, and screenshot evidence where possible.
